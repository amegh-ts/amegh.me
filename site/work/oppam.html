The product

ContextTwo sides of one platform

Oppam is an online counselling platform. Clients find a counsellor, book a session and pay online. Behind that, the Oppam team runs the business from an internal admin panel.

I joined as a Software Developer in December 2025 and work across the stack: PostgreSQL models with Sequelize, Express APIs on Node.js, and the Next.js screens on top.

Payments and pricing

BillingStripe, Razorpay, wallet

A booking isn't done until it's paid for, so this is where mistakes cost the most. I built the payment and pricing systems end to end:

  • Two gateways. Stripe and Razorpay, behind one payment flow.
  • A wallet on the platform.
  • Coupons for discounts.
  • Country-specific pricing.
  • Invoicing.

Admin and HR tools

InternalFor the Oppam team

The admin panel is where the team works every day, so it gets the same care as the client side.

  • HR modules for employee onboarding, payroll, leave and holidays.
  • Role-based permissions across the admin panel, so each person sees and changes only what their role allows.
  • A redesign of key admin screens.

The timezone audit

ReliabilityPlatform-wide

Bookings and reminders only work if every part of the system agrees on what time it is. I led a platform-wide audit of how dates and times are handled, and fixed the booking and reminder bugs it turned up.

A longer write-up of this audit is planned for the blog.

Testing

QualityCritical flows

Payment and booking changes are risky to ship by eye. I wrote Vitest tests and Playwright end-to-end tests for the critical flows, so they're checked automatically instead of by hand.
